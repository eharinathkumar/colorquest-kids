# ColorQuest Kids phased curriculum roadmap

This roadmap guides the long game beyond the profile-and-creativity foundations. Version 2.0 begins the mentor layer and the hands-on Science Lab.

## Released in version 2.0

- Gentle recommendations based on age, completed work, and voluntary interest signals
- Named Math and Science paths, with repeat/skip freedom and no streak pressure
- Alternate explanations, visual concept stories, improved hints, real-world relevance, and reflection
- 32 Science Labs across four age worlds, each with safety level and scientific-method structure
- Curated grown-up-gated learning links and an interest-aware book shelf
- Stronger reasoning explanations in matching and other puzzle families

## Phase 1 — foundations

- Math: number sense, counting, shapes, patterns, comparison, and position
- Science: observation, senses, living things, matter, Earth, weather, and space

## Phase 2 — operations and measurement

- Math: addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, units, time, and money
- Science: measurement, forces, motion, light, sound, heat, and energy

## Phase 3 — parts and particles

- Math: fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, and scale
- Science: states of matter, mixtures, particles, atoms, elements, and safe introductory reactions
- Science Lab expansion: add evidence notes and a drawing notebook to the released prediction, steps, observation, explanation, and wonder flow

## Phase 4 — geometry and systems

- Math: lines, angles, symmetry, perimeter, area, volume, coordinates, and map scale
- Science: ecosystems, cells, body systems, weather systems, rocks, erosion, and plate tectonics

## Phase 5 — algebra and physical laws

- Math: unknown quantities, variables, expressions, equations, functions, probability, and data
- Science: Newton’s laws, gravity, electricity, magnetism, waves, and energy transfer

## Phase 6 — deeper investigations

- Math: multi-step equations, proportional reasoning, statistics, and mathematical modeling
- Science: chemical bonding and reactions, genetics, climate, stars, galaxies, and the evolving universe

Each topic must be rewritten for ages 1–3, 4–6, 7–9, and 10–12. Younger children receive concrete play and visual stories; older children receive models, evidence, vocabulary, calculations, and engineering challenges. Children may repeat, skip, and explore at their own pace.

Physical Science Lab activities must be labeled **Child can try**, **Grown-up nearby**, or **Grown-up required**. Do not include flames, dangerous chemicals, tasting experiment materials, sealed pressure experiments, mains electricity, or other inappropriate hazards.

## Mentor growth plan

1. **Understand:** notice age, completion, attempts, repeats, and child-chosen interests on the device.
2. **Recommend:** offer one small Math step, one Science step, one creative challenge, and an optional book—never a forced route.
3. **Support:** offer a simpler explanation, worked example, visual story, vocabulary help, and safe real-world practice.
4. **Reflect:** invite children to explain, draw, ask a new question, and say whether they want more like it.
5. **Deepen:** connect interests across subjects—for example space → scale → gravity → maps → storytelling.
6. **Share:** give parents understandable progress themes and conversation starters, not rankings or comparisons between children.

## Media plan

- Version 2.0: offline four-frame concept stories for all 64 guided lessons
- Next: short optional narration recorded from original ColorQuest scripts, with captions and playback controls
- Later: 30–90 second original animations for high-value concepts such as place value, fractions, water cycle, forces, atoms, and the solar system
- Do not autoplay sound, embed advertising platforms, or send profile information to video providers

## Content quality rule

Every future lesson must answer six child questions: **What is it? Why does it work? Can I see an example? Can I try it safely? Why does it matter? What can I explore next?** All outside resources and books require editorial review for age fit, accuracy, accessibility, privacy, and commercial pressure.
